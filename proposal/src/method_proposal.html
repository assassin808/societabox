Beyond Learnability: Discrimination-Aware Curricula
for Generative Environments

Haoyang Shang (UBC)  ·  Xuan Liu (UC San Diego)
Research proposal: method

1. Problem

Environment-design methods pick training tasks by a priority score. PAIRED [1] and PLR [2] target regret, but in practice their regret approximations track success rate instead [3]. SFL [3] replaces regret with learnability $p(1-p)$, which picks tasks the agent solves only sometimes, and it works well; its authors note that it assumes deterministic, binary outcomes. LLM self-play task generators show the failure that follows when that assumption breaks. Optimizing a generator for medium difficulty invites reward hacking: it writes ill-posed or unsolvable tasks that look hard [4]. Current fixes are hand-designed binary quality filters. We ask for a principled, continuous priority score that rules these tasks out by construction.

2. Setup: states are items

In a generative environment, states are produced mid-trajectory, and each state $s$ can be treated as a test item. The agent's latent ability is $\theta(w)$, and its outcome at $s$ follows a two-parameter logistic (2PL) model [5]: $p(s,\theta)=\sigma\big(a(s)(\theta-b(s))\big)$, with discrimination $a$ and difficulty $b$. A critical moment is defined as a state with high information about the ability of interest.

3. Results we will develop

(R1) One family. One expected-gradient step on $J_s(w)=p(s,\theta(w))$ moves the ability by
$\Delta\theta\approx\eta\,a\,p(1-p)\,\|\nabla_w\theta\|^2,\qquad I(s)=\dfrac{(\partial_\theta p)^2}{p(1-p)}=a^2p(1-p),\qquad \mathrm{Reg}(s)\approx a\,p(1-p)\,(\theta^\star-\theta).$
All three are members of $\;\mathrm{Pri}_\kappa(s)=a^{\kappa}\,p(1-p)$: learnability ($\kappa{=}0$), learning progress and linearized regret ($\kappa{=}1$), and measurement information ($\kappa{=}2$). Training and evaluation therefore want different curricula; they agree on which items are useless and differ by one power of $a$.
(R2) Learnability is fooled by noise. $p(1-p)$ is maximized by a coin-flip item that ignores ability ($a{=}0$, $p{=}\tfrac12$), so the SFL score cannot separate "at the frontier" from "random". Any $\kappa\ge1$ gives such items zero weight.
(R3) Bad items have signatures. Ill-posed, unsolvable or ability-irrelevant items have $a\approx0$. Mis-keyed items, where the reference answer or verifier is wrong, have $a<0$: stronger agents fail more often. Both are detected without hand-written rules.
(R4) When regret degenerates. Regret carries no signal when the reference agent is not ahead on the dimension the item measures (multidimensional case, $a^{\top}(\theta^\star-\theta)\approx0$). It favours items with $p\approx0,\ p^\star\approx1$, where progress is near zero, and it is biased upward on noise items.

In the multidimensional case, $p=\sigma(a(s)^{\top}\theta-b)$, and progress along a target direction $u$ is $p(1-p)\,a(s)^{\top}Gu$ with $G=\nabla_w\theta\,\nabla_w\theta^{\top}$. A useful state is one whose discrimination points along the agent's deficiency, and the off-diagonal entries of $G$ capture transfer and interference between abilities.

4. Method

Estimating $a,b$ at the state level. Estimating $a$ requires examinees of different ability, which a single agent cannot supply. The checkpoints saved during training provide such a population for free. Because a generative environment can rewind, we branch at a state $s$ and let $M$ checkpoints respond from the same point. This is impossible in the real world, and it makes the 2PL parameters of individual states estimable. Checkpoint abilities are anchored on a fixed item bank, so that $a$ measures ability rather than training time.
Generator reward. $r_G(s)=\max(a(s),0)\,p(1-p)$ for training ($\kappa{=}1$), and $a^2p(1-p)$ for adaptive evaluation ($\kappa{=}2$). Items with negative $a$ are logged as suspected mis-keys. The agent's training algorithm is held fixed.

5. Experiments

E1: minimal falsificationA controlled environment family mixed with planted noise items ($a{=}0$) and mis-keyed items ($a{<}0$). Prediction: SFL and entropy-based priorities over-sample the noise items; $\mathrm{Pri}_{1}$ does not. We also measure how accurately R3 flags the planted items.
E2: UED benchmarkJAX minimax mazes, with a stochastic-tile variant to break SFL's determinism assumption. Baselines: DR, PAIRED, PLR, ACCEL, SFL. Metric: zero-shot transfer.
E3: LLM task generatorA self-play task generator for reasoning tasks, compared against the binary quality filter of [4] at an equal generation budget.

Risks. Checkpoints also drift in nuisance properties such as verbosity and format, so we use an anchor bank and multidimensional IRT to separate them. States within one trajectory are not locally independent, so we treat a critical event and its follow-up turns as a testlet. Neighbouring work [6,7] uses IRT difficulty or regret-trained LLM environment designers; neither estimates state-level discrimination or relates the priority scores to one another.
