Where Is the Agent Socially Deficient? Co-Evolving LLM Agents
and a Generative Social Sandbox through Critical Moments

Haoyang Shang (UBC)  ·  Xuan Liu (UC San Diego)
Joint course project proposal: Track A for the Reinforcement Learning course, Track B for the Machine Learning course (UBC). One method, one paper.

1. Problem and insight

Teaching an LLM agent a long-horizon social ability (leading a team, sustaining a relationship) needs scarce real data or full rollouts in a simulator that reproduces the human data-generating process, although most steps are routine. Our prior work found that social outcomes hinge on a few low-entropy critical moments [1], and that social behaviour is governed by a few programmable meta-attributes, such as bias strengths, set through prompts or steering/task vectors [2,3]. We turn these findings into a training principle: train only where it matters, only on the meta-attributes that are missing, in a sandbox that itself learns to create those moments. Both the agent and the sandbox are trainable.

2. Formulation

A long-horizon POMDP has horizon $H$ and trajectories $\tau$. The agent's behaviour is summarized by meta-attributes $m\in\mathbb R^d$ (bias strengths, risk attitude, theory-of-mind depth, persona consistency). The target ability has unknown $m^\star$; the deficiency is $\Delta=m^\star-m$.

(H1) Temporal sparsity [1]. $G(\tau)=\sum_{t\in C(\tau)} r_t+c$ with $|C(\tau)|\le K\ll H$ critical steps. (H2) Decisiveness [1]. $\mathcal H(\pi(\cdot\mid s))\le\delta$ for $s\in C$, so one rollout per critical moment is informative. (H3) Attribute sparsity (trait activation [4]). The verifier outcome at a critical moment $c$ is $y_c=\psi(c)^{\top}\Delta+\xi$ with $\|\psi(c)\|_0\le s$: each moment activates only a few meta-attributes. The deficiency is $k$-sparse, $\|\Delta\|_0\le k\ll d$.

Compressed episodes. Under H1, an episode is equivalent to a jump process $(z_0,c_1,a_1,z_1,\dots,c_K,a_K)$. The sandbox's jump model $W_\phi(z_{j}\mid z_{j-1},a_{j-1})$ summarizes each routine segment as $z_j$, routine steps run on a frozen script policy (options [5]), and the agent acts only at the $c_j$: $O(K)$ agent calls instead of $O(H)$.

3. Method: critical-moment co-evolution

The agent $A_w$ is a frozen LLM plus a small controller that sets $m_t$ via steering/task vectors (optionally LoRA), only at critical moments. The sandbox $G_\phi$ is an LLM world engine whose actions are moment specifications $c=(\text{context},\text{trigger},\text{probed attributes})$ and summaries $z$. The verifier $V$ is calibrated on real human data. Training alternates:

Sense. $G_\phi$ proposes $n$ moments with sparse designs $\Psi=[\psi(c_1),\dots,\psi(c_n)]^{\top}$. The deficiency is recovered by $\hat\Delta=\arg\min\|\Delta\|_1$ s.t. $\|y-\Psi\Delta\|_1\le\eta$. Expander-graph sparse designs satisfy RIP-1, giving $\ell_1$ recovery from $n=O(k\log(d/k))$ moments [6]: trait activation becomes a testable sensing-matrix property.
Teach. $G_\phi$ generates moments on $\operatorname{supp}(\hat\Delta)$ at the agent's frontier, and the agent is updated by policy gradient (GRPO or PPO) on critical steps alone.
Calibrate. A few full rollouts and human data check that compression preserves outcomes; $W_\phi$ and the moment detector are updated.

The sandbox is trained by RL with a dense reward:

$r_G=\alpha\,\underbrace{I(\Delta;\,y_c\mid y_{\text{past}})}_{\text{sense}}+\beta\,\underbrace{\big[J_{c}(w_{t+1})-J_{c}(w_t)\big]}_{\text{teach}}-\lambda\,\underbrace{D\big(P_{\text{full}}(G)\,\|\,P_{\text{comp}}(G)\big)}_{\text{faithfulness}} .$

Target result (to prove, extending Thm. 1 of [1]): if each jump has outcome error at most $\varepsilon_W$, then $|J_{\text{full}}(\pi)-J_{\text{comp}}(\pi)|\le K(R_{\max}\varepsilon_W+L\delta)$. Compressed training is provably close to full training at a fraction of the cost.

4. What is new

vs. PAIRED/PLR/ACCEL [7,8]: they generate full environments, estimate regret per environment with full rollouts, and give the generator a sparse regret reward; we generate compressed episodes of critical moments, separate measuring from teaching, and use a dense reward with a faithfulness term. vs. SOTOPIA-π [9]: whole-dialogue training, no diagnosis. vs. jumpy world models/options: our jump points are chosen by a trainable sandbox. vs. synthetic-population benchmarks [10]: they diagnose; we diagnose and repair.

5. Two tracks

Track A: algorithm (RL)A synthetic sparse-critical POMDP suite with controllable $(H,K,d,k,s)$ and known $\Delta$, to test H1–H3, the recovery bound and the compression bound. Long-horizon text games (TextWorld, ALFWorld) with an LLM jump model. Baselines: full-rollout PPO/GRPO, DR, PAIRED, Robust PLR. Metrics: recovery error vs. $n$, return vs. agent calls, faithfulness. No social content needed.
Track B: social sandbox (ML)6–10 meta-attributes from [2] as steering/task vectors; a moment detector trained by counterfactual turn resampling, $\iota_t=\mathbb E[V\mid\text{resample }t]-V$; a sandbox of script graphs, perturbations and NPC meta-attributes (visualized by our town demo). Evaluation: SOTOPIA(-hard) goal completion; faithfulness on the speed-dating and divorce data of [1]; human ratings on a subset.

Questions. Q1: do H1–H3 hold (sparsity of $\Psi$, concentration of advantage)? Q2: at equal agent calls, does co-evolution beat full-rollout training and PAIRED/PLR? Q3: is $\hat\Delta$ interpretable and stable across seeds? Risks. If H3 fails, use dense designs with LASSO; for a noisy verifier, average judges and check against human ratings. Plan. Wk 1–3 synthetic suite, attribute vectors; 4–7 Sense/Teach (A), detector (B); 8–11 full co-evolution; 12–13 write-up, demo.
