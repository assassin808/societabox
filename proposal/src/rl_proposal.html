Compressed Regret Sensing: Environment Design without
Reproducing the Data-Generating Process

Haoyang Shang (UBC)  ·  Xuan Liu (UC San Diego)
Course project proposal, Reinforcement Learning (UBC). Companion to the Machine Learning course proposal; both form one paper.

1. Motivation

Training self-improving, long-horizon agents with RL needs either scarce real interaction data or full rollouts in a simulator that reproduces the whole data-generating process, which is wasteful because most steps of a long episode are routine. Unsupervised environment design (UED) avoids the data problem by generating training environments: PAIRED [1] trains an adversary to maximize the agent's regret, and PLR/ACCEL [2,3] replay and edit high-regret levels. All of them estimate regret one environment at a time with full rollouts, and PAIRED's generator gets one sparse reward per environment. We ask two questions: how few rollouts are needed to know where an agent is deficient, and which parts of an episode need to be rolled out at all?

2. Setting

An underspecified POMDP $\mathcal M=\langle A,O,\Theta,S,T,I,R,\gamma\rangle$ yields a POMDP $\mathcal M_\theta$ for each free parameter $\theta\in\Theta$. Let $V(\pi,\theta)$ be the expected discounted return and $\mathrm{Reg}(\pi,\theta)=V(\pi^\star_\theta,\theta)-V(\pi,\theta)$. PAIRED approximates $\pi^\star_\theta$ with an antagonist; at Nash equilibrium the protagonist is minimax-regret. We assume access only to a small evaluation set $\mathcal E=\{\tilde\theta_1,\dots,\tilde\theta_m\}\sim P^\star$ with a verifier, and no generator of $P^\star$.

3. Structural assumptions: two kinds of sparsity

(A1) Skill sparsity. There exist a skill-demand map $\phi:\Theta\to\mathbb R^d$ and a deficiency vector $\delta(\pi)\in\mathbb R^d$ with
$\mathrm{Reg}(\pi,\theta)=\phi(\theta)^{\top}\delta(\pi)+\varepsilon,\qquad \|\delta(\pi)\|_0\le k\ll d .$
Equivalently, the regret matrix $R_{ij}=\mathrm{Reg}(\pi_i,\theta_j)$ over a population of agents and environments has rank $\le d$.
(A2) Temporal sparsity. In an $H$-step episode, $\big|\{t:\ |A^{\pi}(s_t,a_t)|>\epsilon\}\big|\le s\ll H$: only a few decision-critical steps carry the learning signal.

4. Method

(a) Randomize to measure. Pick $n$ probe environments and measure $y_i=\hat V(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}},\theta_i)-\hat V(\pi,\theta_i)$, where $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the antagonist or the best agent in the population. Recover the deficiency by basis pursuit:

$\hat\delta=\arg\min_{\delta}\|\delta\|_1\ \ \text{s.t.}\ \ \|y-\Phi\delta\|_2\le\eta,\qquad \Phi=[\phi(\theta_1),\dots,\phi(\theta_n)]^{\top}.$

If $\Phi$ satisfies the RIP of order $2k$ with $\delta_{2k}<\sqrt2-1$, then $\|\hat\delta-\delta\|_2\le C_0k^{-1/2}\|\delta-\delta_k\|_1+C_1\eta$ [4], and for sub-Gaussian designs $n=O(k\log(d/k))$ probes suffice. Domain randomization is a poor training distribution but a good sensing design; we use it only to measure.

(b) Learn $\phi$ by regret-matrix completion. $\phi$ is unknown for rich $\Theta$. A population of agents gives sparse entries $\Omega$ of $R$; we solve $\min_X\|X\|_*$ s.t. $P_\Omega(X)=P_\Omega(\hat R)$ (or factorized ALS), which needs $|\Omega|=O(r(N+M)\,\mathrm{polylog})$ entries instead of $NM$ [5]. A regressor $g_\omega(\theta)\approx\phi(\theta)$ extends it to unseen $\theta$.

(c) Target to train. The generator $G_\psi$, an RL policy that writes $\theta$ step by step as in PAIRED, receives a dense predicted-regret reward with a solvability term:

$r_G(\theta)=g_\omega(\theta)^{\top}\hat\delta\;-\;\lambda\,\mathbb 1\!\left[\hat V(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}},\theta)<\tau\right].$

This replaces PAIRED's sparse reward and removes per-environment antagonist rollouts; $\hat\delta$ is re-sensed every $K$ updates.

(d) Sparse rollouts. Flag critical steps by ensemble value disagreement or policy entropy, run routine segments with cached macro-actions (options) [6], and branch Monte-Carlo rollouts only at flagged states: cost $O(sKL)$ instead of $O(NH)$ for $K$ branches of length $L$.

5. Experiments

TestbedsJAX minimax library: MiniGrid mazes (the PAIRED benchmark) with zero-shot transfer to Labyrinth, FourRooms and SixteenRooms. For long horizons, Craftax.
BaselinesDomain randomization, minimax adversary, PAIRED, Robust PLR, ACCEL.
MetricsZero-shot solve rate vs. environment steps; error in $\hat\delta$ vs. number of probes $n$; singular spectrum of $\hat R$; fraction of steps flagged critical and compute saved.

H1 (tests A1): the top $k$ singular values of $\hat R$ capture most of its energy. H2: CRS matches PAIRED/PLR transfer with substantially fewer environment steps. H3 (tests A2): sparse rollouts reduce simulation cost on Craftax without lowering return. Risk: if $R$ is not low-rank, we report the spectrum as a finding and fall back to PLR-style replay prioritized by predicted regret.

6. Plan

Wk 1–3 reproduce PAIRED/PLR in minimax; 4–6 probes, matrix completion, test A1; 7–10 CRS generator, sparse rollouts; 11–13 transfer experiments, write-up. Deliverables: code, report, method section of the joint paper.
