Where Is the Agent Socially Deficient? Low-Rank Skill Sensing
for LLM Agents in a Generative Social Sandbox

Haoyang Shang (UBC)  ·  Xuan Liu (UC San Diego)
Course project proposal, Machine Learning (UBC). Companion to the Reinforcement Learning course proposal; both form one paper.

1. Motivation

Social abilities such as small talk, negotiation and leadership have no rule-based simulator and little real training data, and reproducing the human data-generating process is infeasible. What we can get is a small evaluation set of social tasks with a verifier: SOTOPIA-style goal-completion scores [1] or human ratings. Everyday social life is also highly compressible: most of it follows cultural scripts (ordering, greeting, a class), and individual differences show up at a few choice points [2]. LLM-based synthetic populations are known to answer too consistently and to distort relationships between traits [3]. We turn these observations into an ML problem: learn a low-dimensional map of which social skills a scenario demands and which ones an agent lacks, from a few (agent, scenario) scores.

2. The social sandbox as an underspecified POMDP

A scenario $\theta=(\sigma,\xi,g_{1:n})$ has three parts:

  • a script graph $\sigma$, whose nodes are options (roles, stages, norms, exits) [4];
  • a perturbation $\xi$, either a break (something disrupts the script) or an opportunity (a cue that can trigger a new goal);
  • cognitive genomes $g_j\in[0,1]^p$ for the non-player characters. Each coordinate is one mechanism, such as loss aversion, conformity or social anxiety, calibrated on behavioural assays (Psych-101, choices13k). It is expressed in a frozen LLM through a prompt template or a steering vector, $h_\ell\leftarrow h_\ell+\textstyle\sum_{c}g_{jc}\,v_c$.

The agent $\pi_w$ is an LLM with a LoRA adapter $w$. It observes the dialogue and scene, acts with utterances and moves, and is scored by a verifier $J(\tau)\in[0,10]$. We release the sandbox as a Gym-style multi-agent text environment. The pixel-art town demo is its visual front end, and it currently runs on hand-set rules, not on trained models.

3. Learning problem

Regret matrix. A population of $N$ agents (checkpoints, base models, prompting strategies) is scored on $M$ scenarios, with $R_{ij}=J(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}},\theta_j)-J(\pi_i,\theta_j)$. Only a sparse set of entries $\Omega$ is observed, because each entry costs a full multi-turn rollout.

Inductive matrix completion with sparse deficiencies. We learn a scenario encoder $\phi_\omega(\theta)=f_\omega(\mathrm{text}(\sigma,\xi),g_{1:n})\in\mathbb R^d$ and agent deficiency vectors $u_i$:

$\min_{\omega,U}\ \sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega}\big(R_{ij}-u_i^{\top}\phi_\omega(\theta_j)\big)^2+\lambda\|U\|_F^2+\mu\sum_i\|u_i\|_1 .$

Side information (the text and genomes) lets the model generalize to unseen scenarios, which plain completion [5] cannot do. For a new agent we probe $n$ scenarios and recover its deficiency with a LASSO; the RL companion project analyses $n=O(k\log(d/k))$ under RIP-type conditions.

Targeted scenario generation. An LLM scenario writer is conditioned on a target demand vector $\phi^\dagger\propto\hat u$. Its outputs pass an admission classifier, trained to reject implausible scenarios, and are ranked by predicted regret $\phi_\omega(\theta)^{\top}\hat u$. The agent is then fine-tuned on the admitted scenarios.

Critical-turn detection (temporal compression). For logged dialogues we measure counterfactual turn influence, $\Delta_t=\mathbb E\,[J\mid \text{resample turn }t]-J$, and train a cheap classifier to predict high $|\Delta_t|$ from context. Training focuses on the predicted critical turns, and routine script segments reuse cached transcripts instead of fresh rollouts.

4. Evaluation

DataSOTOPIA scenarios and its hard subset; our genome-parameterized NPC partners; behavioural assays for calibrating the genomes.
ModelsQwen2.5-1.5B/7B-Instruct with LoRA as agents; a frozen LLM with steering vectors for NPCs.
BaselinesUniformly random scenarios (domain randomization); SOTOPIA-$\pi$-style behaviour cloning plus self-reinforcement [6]; PLR-style replay of high-score-gap scenarios.
MetricsGoal completion on held-out hard scenarios; RMSE of held-out entries of $R$ vs. $|\Omega|$; number of probes needed to recover $\hat u$; canonical correlation between learned axes and genome dimensions; training compute saved by critical-turn selection; spread of NPC behaviour vs. a single-persona baseline.

Q1 Is $R$ approximately low-rank for social tasks? Q2 Do the learned skill axes line up with cognitive mechanisms, making skills readable and editable? Q3 Does targeted generation beat random and replay baselines at equal rollout budget? Risk: verifier noise can inflate the rank of $R$. We average several verifier samples and report agreement with human ratings on a subset.

5. Plan

Wk 1–3 text sandbox API, scripts, genome expression for 4–6 mechanisms; 4–6 agent population, sparse score matrix, test Q1; 7–10 inductive completion, admission classifier, targeted generation, critical-turn detection; 11–13 held-out evaluation, write-up, demo. Joint paper: the RL project supplies the method and theory (MiniGrid, Craftax); this project supplies the social application.
